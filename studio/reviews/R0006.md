## Jam Critic

First review: there is no earlier one, so every trend is `new`. Evidence: fresh shots taken this run (`./scripts/shots.sh <slug>` with frames 30/240/900 for all four games, plus `./scripts/shots.sh hub`), each game's DESIGN.md and game.json, and a grep of the Synth calls in each game's code. I couldn't hear anything, so audio is scored from the code.

### Collection

| Overall | Gameplay | Graphics | Audio | Innovation | Theme |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 6.6 | 6.5 | 7.0 | 6.3 | 6.4 | 7.8 |

- **Overall (6.6):** It already feels like one entry. All four games share the BG/TRACE circuit grid, the same HUD stack (40 px score, three accent life squares, one progress bar) and the same HUB/SOUND corners, and the hub's register of `0` cells looks like a product and not a menu. What holds it back is that 4 small games, each 30 to 75 s long, with no finale yet, read as "a promising demo reel". OVERFLOW and the next 1 to 2 games are what turn this into a voter's "oh, clever".
- **Theme (7.8):** This is the strongest pitch in the build. "One word read eight ways, inside a literal shift register" is clear at a glance, because SHAPE / GEAR / KEY / BIT appear under the cells, and each game's only verb is its SHIFT. Bit Catcher (a real `rol8`/`ror8`) and Upper Case (the actual Shift key) are clever readings. Shapeshifter and Gear Grind are the obvious ones, which is fine as openers.
- Graphics are cohesive and on-palette everywhere, with no glyph boxes in any shot. Audio follows the bible closely (see per game), but the `hit` preset is used nowhere, and two games have no custom tone at all, so the collection's sound palette risks feeling samey across 4 games.

**Shapeshifter (slot 1, SHAPE)**
- *Best:* the tunnel. Walls swell from the vanishing point, the next wall shows through the current hole, and the glowing pink player shape reads instantly. The `start` shot alone teaches the game, the hint glow on the matching button (play-240) does what Pillar 2 asks, and the `+10` popup and burst sit right.
- *Worst:* the last instant before impact. In play-900 the arriving wall is a flat PANEL slab covering about 55% of the screen, and the mismatch (square player, triangle hole) reads as "a triangle drawn on a square", not as "you're about to crash". The BAD warning pulses at 4 Hz, so its off phase can be what the player sees in the critical 0.3 s. Audio is all bible presets plus one morph tone, with a 132 bpm square loop that restarts transposed in RUSH. Solid but not distinctive.
- *Ship bar:* **meets** (7.2, no category below 5).

**Gear Grind (slot 2, GEAR)**
- *Best:* the tach and the engine. The amber band with its GOOD perfect core, the needle drop on a shift, the `PERFECT +30` popup with boost flame and speed lines (play-240), and in code an engine `Synth.tone` that follows rpm with a rev-limiter stutter over a low saw loop. That's the best audio design in the set.
- *Worst:* no sense of a race. The player car is pinned on screen and the rival sits level with it in both play shots. The road is an empty PANEL band with dashes, so a still frame (and most moments of play) looks parked. The staging lights end in GOOD yellow, so GO reads like "caution". The gameplay is 2 to 4 timing presses per ~8 s heat, which is thin by itself.
- *Ship bar:* **meets** (6.8), with the least headroom on Innovation (5.8).

**Upper Case (slot 3, KEY)**
- *Best:* the reading of the theme. You press the real Shift key, on the opposite hand the way a touch typist does, the tiles fall above their own key on a drawn QWERTY strip, and the target key lights up. Literal, witty and unmistakable.
- *Worst:* the hint glow on the Shift keys is built by scaling the key rect 1.3x and 1.6x (`keys_draw.gd` `_glow`). Because the key is 460 px wide, the glow becomes a ~740 px teal slab from x 32 to about 770 that runs **under the opposite Shift key** (play-240, play-900). It looks like a rendering bug and blurs which Shift is being hinted, which is the one thing the hint must make clear. Audio has no custom tone at all, only presets and a triangle loop, with letters on the beat.
- *Ship bar:* **meets** (6.6), but only just. The glow bug is the cheapest fix in the whole collection.

**Bit Catcher (slot 4, BIT)**
- *Best:* the idea. The register really rotates (`rol8`/`ror8`), it echoes the hub's own register, and it's the collection's only puzzle. It has the highest Innovation and Theme scores here.
- *Worst:* you can't see the state. In every shot the register shows eight `0`s. Sockets are lime outlines and set bits don't read as `1`s in the cells, so after 15 s of play (score 566) the register still looks empty. Progress lives in an unexplained `11110000` digit string under the score, which clashes with the register's own meaning. The middle of the screen (y 130 to 460) is empty except for one falling tile. Graphics are the weakest in the set.
- *Ship bar:* **below**. Overall 6.3 < 6.5, though no category is below 5. It can be fixed in 2 tasks (fixes 2 and 3 below).

### Cut watch
- **Bit Catcher (6.3):** below the ship bar, with no category under 5. **Fix, don't cut.** Its theme reading (8.2) is the best in the set and it mirrors the hub. Its problem is presentation (fixes 2 and 3, about 2 tasks), not a weak concept. Re-score after those fixes; if it's still under 6.5, cut it in POLISH only once at least 5 other games are shipped.
- **Upper Case (6.6)** and **Gear Grind (6.8):** above the bar but with little margin. Fixes 1 and 5 are cheap insurance.
- With 4 games in the hub and `min_games` = 5, no cut is possible right now anyway. The next shipped game (Phase Shift is scaffolded) is what unlocks the OVERFLOW finale.
